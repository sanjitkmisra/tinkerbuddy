TinkerBuddy

TinkerBuddy for schools and IT

For teachers, technology coordinators and privacy reviewers. TinkerBuddy is an independent product, not affiliated with or endorsed by Autodesk. Tinkercad is a trademark of Autodesk, Inc.

TinkerBuddy is a Chrome and Microsoft Edge extension that coaches students aged about 8–14 while they design in Tinkercad. It plans the project with them, points at the right button and checks each step. It cannot click, drag, type or change anything in the design: the student does the work.

Install it for your students

On managed Chromebooks and school computers students usually cannot add extensions themselves, so IT installs TinkerBuddy for them. Extension IDs:

  • Chrome Web Store: listed here once the Chrome Web Store approves TinkerBuddy; until then, ask us
  • Microsoft Edge Add-ons: listed here once Microsoft Edge Add-ons approves TinkerBuddy; until then, ask us

Google Admin console (Chromebooks and managed Chrome)

  1. Go to Devices → Chrome → Apps & extensions → Users & browsers, and choose the organizational unit for your students (and teachers, so they can try it).
  2. Add a Chrome app or extension by ID, enter the Chrome Web Store ID above, and choose From the Chrome Web Store.
  3. Set the installation policy to Force install (or Force install + pin to browser toolbar). Save.

If your domain blocks extensions by the permissions they request, the list TinkerBuddy asks for is below. If student accounts are set as under 18, Google's third-party access settings may also need TinkerBuddy allowed for those accounts.

Microsoft Edge (Intune or Group Policy)

Add this value to the ExtensionInstallForcelist policy (Intune: Settings catalog → Microsoft Edge → Extensions → "Control which extensions are installed silently"):

<Edge Add-ons ID>;https://edge.microsoft.com/extensionwebstorebase/v1/crx

Google Chrome on Windows or Mac (Group Policy, Intune or a configuration profile)

Add this value to Chrome's ExtensionInstallForcelist policy:

<Chrome Web Store ID>;https://clients2.google.com/service/update2/crx

TinkerBuddy needs Chrome or Edge version 120 or later, on a laptop or desktop. Tablets and phones aren't supported.

Voice input, if a teacher switches it on, uses the browser's own speech service (Google for Chrome, Microsoft for Edge). If that is blocked, voice simply doesn't work and students type instead.

Running a class

  1. The teacher creates a class on tinkerbuddy.app with their school email and gets a class code and a private dashboard.
  2. Students open TinkerBuddy's Settings and type the class code. Only then does their learning progress reach the teacher's dashboard.
  3. The teacher chooses the switches: daily AI limits, voice input, workspace pictures, or pausing AI answers for everyone.

New classes get a free 6-week pilot for up to 35 students, 20 AI answers per student per day. After that, the Teacher plan is $299 a year per teacher, covering all their classes (up to 150 students). Schools and districts: by invoice, about $5 per student a year. Purchase orders welcome.

Student privacy law

  • COPPA. TinkerBuddy is designed to comply with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act. For school use, the school consents on parents' behalf for this educational purpose only, as the FTC's guidance permits; we use student data only to provide TinkerBuddy to the school, never commercially. We recommend that the school or district, not only a single teacher, approves TinkerBuddy.
  • FERPA. We act as a school official with a legitimate educational interest, under the school's direct control of student data, and delete it on request.
  • State laws and agreements. We will sign your state's or district's data privacy agreement. Our retention policy is in the privacy policy; a security one-pager is available on request.

AI and safety

  • Answers come from Anthropic's Claude through our server. Anthropic does not use this data to train its models, and we don't store questions, answers or pictures.
  • TinkerBuddy tells students it is an AI program, not a person.
  • It only answers about building in Tinkercad, never does the design for the student, refuses unsafe requests, and if a student says they are hurt, scared or bullied, tells them kindly to talk to a teacher, parent or another trusted adult. These behaviours are tested before every release.
